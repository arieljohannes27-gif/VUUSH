## Authority

This document defines how VUUSH speaks.

It applies to product UI, notifications, support, operations tools, sales, marketing, and executive communication. It implements the Brand Foundation in language.

Where this document and older voice outlines conflict, **this document wins**.

Do not invent brand jargon in the product. Especially: do not say “The Point” to end users unless a rare, deliberate brand moment requires it. The Point guides *how* we write commit moments — it is not a customer slogan.

---

## 1. Voice (constant)

VUUSH’s voice does not change by channel. Tone flexes; voice does not.

| Attribute | Meaning in language |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Clear** | One idea per sentence. Say what is true. |
| **Calm** | Steady under pressure. No panic punctuation. |
| **Precise** | Exact verbs for exact states. |
| **Human** | Respect time and dignity. No condescension. |
| **Quietly confident** | We do not oversell. We do not hedge into mush. |
| **Intelligent** | Helpful and specific — never clever for its own sake. |

**Voice in one line:** Composed, exact, human — effortless, not rushed.

---

## 2. Tone (flexes by moment)

Tone shifts with the phase of commitment. Personality stays the same.

| Moment | Tone | Goal |
|--------|------|------|
| **Before The Point** | Open, light, reversible | Safe to explore |
| **At The Point** | Direct, unmistakable | Clear commitment |
| **After The Point** | Steady, informative | Trust we are carrying it |
| **When something fails** | Honest, blame-free, actionable | Path forward |
| **When complete** | Quiet, resolved | Time returned — no fireworks |
| **Emergency / threat** | Sparse, practical, non-judgemental | Help first |
| **Marketing / brand** | Confident, understated | Promise without hype |
| **Enterprise / sales** | Precise, credible | Substance over theatre |

## 3. Writing principles

1. **Short by default** — Cut words that do not change meaning or next action.  
2. **Lead with the fact** — Status first; explanation second; next step third.  
3. **Name the action exactly** — Prefer *Confirm*, *Accept*, *Send*, *Pay* over vague *Submit* / *Continue* when commitment happens.  
4. **Separate draft from real** — Soft states sound soft (*Draft*, *Quote*, *Not sent yet*). Committed states sound committed (*Confirmed*, *Assigned*, *Paid*).  
5. **No blame language** — Describe the situation and the path. Do not accuse the user, driver, or “the system” theatrically.  
6. **No urgency theatre** — Do not manufacture stress to force action.  
7. **Honesty over comfort fiction** — If we do not know location, ETA, or status, say so calmly.  
8. **One ecosystem standard** — Drivers and ops get the same clarity and respect as customers — denser where needed, never careless.  
9. **Return time** — Every sentence should earn its place. If it doesn’t help decide, act, or understand, remove it.

## 4. Language by moment

### 4.1 Before The Point (explore / draft)

**Feel:** Safe. Nothing unintended happens.

**Do:** Soft labels. Clear that nothing is final yet.  
**Don’t:** Language that sounds already booked or paid.

| Prefer | Avoid |
|--------|--------|
| Get a quote | Book now (if not yet committing) |
| Draft delivery | Your delivery is on the way |
| Review details | Finalise instantly |
| Not confirmed yet | You’re all set |

**Example**

> Review pickup and dropoff. You can change these before you confirm.

---

### 4.2 At The Point (commit)

**Feel:** Clarity and confidence.

**Do:** One primary verb. State what becomes real.  
**Don’t:** Soften a hard commit with playful or ambiguous copy.

| Moment | Prefer | Avoid |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Customer pays / confirms | Confirm delivery | Place order!!! |
| Driver takes job | Accept job | Grab this one |
| Payment | Pay · Confirm payment | Smash that pay button |
| Irreversible-ish | This starts the delivery | Let’s gooo |

**Example**

> Confirm delivery  
> You’ll be charged and we’ll assign a driver.

**Example (driver)**

> Accept job  
> Once you accept, this delivery is yours to complete.

---

### 4.3 After The Point (in progress)

**Feel:** Steady trust.

**Do:** Truthful status. Short updates. What happens next when useful.  
**Don’t:** Fake precision, cheerleading, or filler reassurance.

| Prefer | Avoid |
|--------|--------|
| Driver assigned · En route to pickup | Your hero is speeding your way! |
| Waiting for a live location | Almost there! (when unknown) |
| Delivery paused — we’re on it | Something magical is happening |

**Example**

> Confirmed. We’re assigning a driver.  
> You’ll see updates here as the delivery moves.

**Example (tracking, no live signal)**

> Last known location is unavailable right now.  
> We’ll show movement when we have a reliable signal.

---

### 4.4 When something fails

**Feel:** Honesty without blame theatre.

**Structure:** What happened → What it means → What to do next (or what we’re doing).

| Prefer | Avoid |
|--------|--------|
| We couldn’t complete delivery — recipient unavailable | You failed the dropoff |
| Payment didn’t go through. Try again or use another method | Payment error E_92 |
| Signal lost. Job stays with you until reconnect | GPS broke lol |

**Example**

> We couldn’t deliver — no one was available at the address.  
> You can reschedule or contact support. Your payment status is unchanged until this is resolved.

---

### 4.5 When complete

**Feel:** Quiet resolution.

**Do:** Confirm done. Offer the next useful action only if needed.  
**Don’t:** Confetti copy, points theatre, or hype.

| Prefer | Avoid |
|--------|--------|
| Delivered | BOOM delivered!!! |
| Proof saved | You’re amazing |

**Example**

> Delivered.  
> Proof is saved on this delivery.

---

### 4.6 Emergency

**Feel:** Sparse. Practical. Non-judgemental.

**Do:** Help first. Clear next physical action.  
**Don’t:** Corporate softness that slows help; never imply the person did something wrong by needing help.

**Example**

> Get to safety first.  
> Call local emergency services if you need them.  
> Then tell us what’s happening — we’ll pause the delivery and alert dispatch.

## 5. Channel notes

### Product UI

- Labels over paragraphs.  
- Buttons carry the commit verb.  
- Body text explains consequence, not personality.  
- Same voice in customer, driver, dispatch, support, and admin — density may differ.

### Notifications

- Fact in the first line.  
- Deep link or next step in the second if needed.  
- No marketing voice in operational alerts.

### Support

- Acknowledge → clarify → resolve or next step.  
- Mirror the user’s words when helpful.  
- Never hide behind policy tone when a clear answer exists.

### Marketing & brand

- Lead with time returned and calm completion — not “fastest courier.”  
- Understated confidence. Specific claims only when true.  
- The square / The Point: brand films and decks may use them; ads should not require a lecture.

### Enterprise / sales

- Outcomes, integrity of process, clarity of commitment.  
- Prefer proof and precision over visionary fog.

## 6. Word list

### Prefer

| Use | Why |
|-----|-----|
| Confirm / Accept / Send / Pay | Clear Point verbs |
| Delivery / job (by role) | Plain |
| Assigned / En route / Delivered | Exact states |
| Paused / On hold | Honest interruption |
| We’ll carry this through | Faithful completion (sparingly) |
| Time back / less friction | Brand promise language (marketing; rare in UI) |

### Avoid / ban in product UI

| Don’t use | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| Blazing fast / turbo / rush / ASAP energy | Urgency theatre |
| Smash / grab / snag / hero driver | Gimmick / courier-cliché |
| Oopsie / Whoops / Uh-oh | Undermines precision |
| Something went wrong (alone) | Incomplete — always add meaning or next step when possible |
| Please be patient (alone) | Filler; say what is happening instead |
| Synergy / revolutionary / disrupt | Empty hype |
| The Point / square pixel story | Internal / brand — not UI chrome copy |

### Technology stance (not courier slang)

| Prefer | Avoid as default |
|--------|------------------|
| Platform, delivery, assignment | Run, drop, bang out |
| Driver | Guy / rider (unless market-proven and approved) |
| Proof of delivery | Pic or it didn’t happen |

Role-appropriate shorthand is fine in driver/ops tools when it stays clear and respectful.

## 7. Microcopy patterns

### Buttons

| Context | Pattern |
|---------|---------|
| Soft | Continue · Back · Edit · Save draft |
| Commit | Confirm delivery · Accept job · Pay · Send request |
| Destructive / serious | Cancel delivery · Record failed attempt · Declare emergency |
| Secondary | Not now · Decline · Keep editing |

### Status lines

`[State]. [What it means in one short clause].`  
Optional: `[Next step].`

### Empty / loading / unknown

> Looking up the latest status…  
> No live location yet.  
> Nothing here yet.

Never invent a comforting story to fill the gap.

## 8. Role flavour (same voice)

| Audience | Flavour | Still true |
|----------|---------|------------|
| **Customer** | Reassuring through clarity | No false ETAs |
| **Driver** | Direct, operational, dignified | No slang that demeans |
| **Dispatch / support / admin** | Dense, scannable, exact | No cold contempt for users |

One ecosystem: we do not “talk down” to drivers or “talk up” to executives with a different soul.

## 9. Multilingual

- **Baseline:** English (South Africa–aware plain language).  
- Future locales must preserve calm precision and commit clarity — not literal hustle idioms.  
- Translate meaning and threshold, not slang.

---

## 10. Quick tests

Before shipping copy, ask:

1. **Is The Point obvious** if this is a commit moment?  
2. **Would this waste someone’s time** to read?  
3. **Is it calm and exact** under stress?  
4. **Would we say this to a driver and a customer** with the same respect?  
5. **Does it tell the truth** even when the truth is “we don’t know yet”?
